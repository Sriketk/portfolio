After roughly nine to twelve months at Ally Financial in Charlotte, Sriket quit with nothing lined up — no other job, no co-founder, no committed plan beyond "learn and post on Twitter." It was the bet that opened [[solo-builder-era]].

## What he was running from

The slow accumulation of frustrations from [[ally-financial-era]]:
- Mundane work as he outgrew the team.
- Coworkers who didn't care about learning more — "they didn't care" is the line he keeps coming back to.
- Being assigned onboarding, interviewing, and work distribution at exactly the moment he wanted to be learning, not teaching.
- Meeting load — 3–4 meetings a day eating 3 hours from a 7-hour productive ceiling. Software, to him, is momentum-based — see [[software-momentum-based]].
- Stand-up he hated leading.
- A pace of personal learning that was slowing under the conditions.

## What he was running toward

Tech Twitter had shown him a different world — AI companies forming, ambitious people, real work being done. He felt the pull of startups but believed he wasn't good enough to land at one (he kept seeing roles go to Waterloo kids with four big-tech internships). The realistic alternative wasn't "join a startup" — it was "leave the corporate job and become someone a startup would want."

He framed the math as roughly: catching up via after-work learning would take six months, and he was already short on energy. The only honest move was to quit and take a swing.

## The catalyst trip

He booked a one-week trip to New York City the week before he thought he was going to quit, just to get inspiration and feel the environment. The trip mattered. Around the same time he was recommended the Acquired podcast — the Meta episode, with Mark Zuckerberg's origin story and the broader Silicon Valley myth, landed hard. "I was like wow, this is what life could be. You could do so much."

## The risk frame

Sriket grew up middle class — money wasn't the gating risk. The gating risk was psychological. He describes himself as having been risk-averse and wanting to seek risk specifically to find out what failure felt like. "To truly live you need to look death right in its eyes" became the operative line (see [[look-death-in-eyes]]). He had never hit rock bottom. Life felt like a simulation in which everything that should happen was happening — no variability. Quitting introduced variability on purpose.

## The decision itself

Quit Ally. No job lined up. The thesis: "my learning and my curiosity would outweigh the risk of quitting my job with nothing lined up." Combined with a desire to get out of Charlotte and into NYC, the quit functioned as both a career bet and a setup for the later [[move-to-nyc]].
