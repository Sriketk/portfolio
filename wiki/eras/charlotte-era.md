The eight years from fifth grade through high school that Sriket spent in Charlotte, North Carolina — the first place his family settled long enough for him to put down roots. Spans elementary school, the eighth-grade fitness/identity turn, and the academically intense high school years that shaped his work ethic.

## Arrival and elementary school

The family moved to Charlotte in fifth grade and lived in the Camden Apartments. Sriket attended Hawker Ridge Elementary, where green Reebok ZigTechs were the uniform of the moment and he was, by his own description, "kind of fat." His closest friendship from the year was with a Russian classmate who lived next door — Sriket isn't sure of the last name now but the friendship was real.

Then the family bought their first actual house — newly built, two stories, beautiful — and Sriket switched to Elon Park Elementary. His father was working at Bank of America at the time. From fifth grade through the end of high school, this house was home.

## Middle school

Sriket describes himself as "not a good student until eighth grade." Through middle school he had no friends made through school — phones were spreading through his cohort and he didn't have one. Math was always his strongest subject.

Eighth grade was the turn: he started losing his childhood weight, walking to the YMCA for six- and seven-hour summer workouts. The fitness shift correlated with the social shift — eighth grade was when he started actually making friends.

## Music and culture

Middle school is also when his music taste formed. The Travis Scott era hit hard: "Birds in the Trap Sing McKnight was freaking insane." He calls out the Future / Drake / Travis collab moment (Panda era). He was deep in sneaker YouTube — Qias Omar and Kick Genius were regular watches.

## High school

Sriket made his first real best friend freshman year. He took AP Psychology that year and treated every class with high effort. School did not come easily to him: he had to work harder than peers for the same grades. The high school was competitive and surrounded by smart students, which he names as load-bearing — "the average person is only as smart as the people around them" (see [[average-as-smart-as-peers]]). He felt unable to try academically without falling behind socially.

SAT prep started freshman year — three-hour Saturday and Sunday morning drives to a prep school. He scored ~1420 four times in a row before finally breaking through his senior year. Six SAT attempts total.

He got his first phone mid-sophomore year — the iPhone X. Sophomore year is one of his favorite years on record: many friends, easy good grades, peak physical shape at the time, gym after school every day, international DECA in Atlanta.

During spring break sophomore-ish year, his parents took him to India for a head-shaving ceremony. He was furious — going bald felt socially fatal and he was at the age of first heavily talking to women. The experience became his college essay. He hasn't reread it; suspects it's cringe.

Junior year was the hardest academic year. AP-loaded schedule, first girlfriend, heavy involvement in DECA, FBLA, TSA, and other clubs, too much social time. Grades dropped — his first B's in high school. He remembers the walk home from school after realizing he was mathematically locked out of an A in APES. He stayed strong in math and, somewhat surprisingly to him, Spanish — he attributes that to grammar being "often very mathematical." He stopped working out for the first time. The era also coincides with his adolescent friction phase: he remembers being rude to his mother.

The summer between junior and senior year, he did Uplift Humanity — a two-week trip to India teaching English to impoverished kids. He met a lot of people from New Jersey and Florida on that trip.

Senior year ran similar to junior year. College applications dominated. He applied as a pre-med — until AP Bio half-killed his interest and he switched to computer science (see [[switch-to-cs]]). The pivot came late, so most of his applications still skewed medical. The University of Maryland College Park offered a free application; he applied on that basis alone and it turned out to be the best CS school he got into — see [[maryland-college]].

Throughout high school, Sriket had no real passion. He was focused on grades and friends — making up, he thinks, for the childhood he'd missed during the nomadic years (see [[childhood-nomadic]]).

## Senior spring and COVID

COVID hit mid-senior year and Sriket admits he was happy about it. He hated AP Bio and had already been accepted to college. Second semester became dead weight. He was working at Chipotle and picked up extra hours during lockdown — pay doubled because nobody wanted to work and online orders surged. (He remembers Chipotle stock going up around then.) A breakup around the same time left him needing to fill time. He went to the track daily — two miles, the Chris Heria ab workout, lifting tires. Lots of free hours, nothing to do but train.

## Coming back

Charlotte became home for years again later — Sriket's first job at [[ally-financial-era]] was back in Charlotte after college, and his [[solo-builder-era]] also happened from Charlotte before he moved to NYC.
