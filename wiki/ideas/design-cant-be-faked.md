A claim Sriket arrived at while building [[cobalt]] solo. The argument: design quality is not a single decision you can outsource or shortcut — it is the aggregate of many small decisions, each of which requires focused attention.

## The core claim

"You can't fake design. It either looks good or it doesn't."

Design is a compromise across many small details: subtle background choice, subtle text-to-width ratio, hundreds of micro-tradeoffs. None of them are big-bang fixes. Their aggregate is what people experience as "good design" or "bad design."

## Why it cannot be parallelized

Sriket frames design as the opposite of work that benefits from background agents. To improve a screen, he had to stare at it for five minutes straight with nothing else running — no distraction, no background processing, no AI assistant doing something else in another window. Just sustained attention on one surface.

This pattern overlaps with [[software-momentum-based]]: both claim that certain kinds of work require uninterrupted blocks of focus and degrade sharply when fragmented.

## The payoff

After months of forcing himself through this discipline during [[solo-builder-era]], Sriket believed it paid off. He could tell why things looked off much more easily — and he considered that a skill worth having developed. Design taste, in his view, is built through this kind of slow, unparallelizable attention, not through reading about design.

## Practical implication

For anyone building solo (or trying to lead design at a startup), the implication is sharp: protect long blocks of single-focus time on individual surfaces. Don't treat design as something you can pattern-match your way through.
