A quote Sriket adopted and used to justify the [[quit-ally]] decision. The operative claim: a life without exposure to real failure is suspiciously frictionless and may not be a fully inhabited life. Variance-seeking is therefore a feature, not a bug.

## What it means to him

Sriket frames his pre-quit life as a simulation in which "everything that should happen was happening" — a smooth, on-rails trajectory with no variability. He had never hit rock bottom. He had never really failed at anything that felt large in retrospect.

The belief is that this absence of failure was itself a problem. To know what life is, you have to encounter the edges of it — including the possibility of things not working out.

## Honest framing

Sriket acknowledges the belief sounds "very nihilistic and very pain-seeking." His response: "but that's the truth." He isn't romanticizing suffering — he's claiming that variance-seeking is the only way to know whether you're alive or just running through a script.

## How it acted on him

It pushed him into the [[quit-ally]] decision: quitting without a job lined up was the most efficient available way to introduce real downside risk into his life. The corporate path offered the opposite — guaranteed continuity, predictable trajectory, no rock bottom on the menu.

## Compatibility with other beliefs

Pairs with [[average-as-smart-as-peers]] (both about exposing yourself to higher-friction environments) and contrasts with later [[bootstrap-philosophy]] (which is risk-managed, not risk-seeking). The belief is most active at inflection points, not in steady-state operation.
