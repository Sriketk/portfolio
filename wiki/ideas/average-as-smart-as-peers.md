A belief Sriket attributes to his high school experience and applies recursively across his career decisions. The claim: a person's effective intelligence is bounded by their peer group, so peer-group selection is an upstream lever on personal capability.

## Origin

The belief formed in high school in Charlotte (see [[charlotte-era]]). His school was competitive and densely populated with smart students. He felt he could not stop trying academically without falling behind socially — the social cost of slacking enforced the academic standard.

## How it shows up later

The same logic surfaces in [[ally-financial-era]]. As Sriket outgrew the technical level of his Ally team, he started experiencing a ceiling: questions stopped being answerable, and worse, the absence of curiosity in his coworkers — "they didn't care about learning more" — capped what he could absorb from being around them. The belief implies that staying meant flattening. Leaving was the implication.

It is also the unspoken logic underneath [[move-to-nyc]]: Charlotte's lack of community wasn't just an absence of events, it was an absence of peers who would raise the ceiling.

## Status

A live, load-bearing belief in Sriket's stack. It connects to but is distinct from [[great-things-not-done-alone]] — peer selection is about who you stand next to; collaboration is about who you build with.
