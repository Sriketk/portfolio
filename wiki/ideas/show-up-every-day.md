A belief Sriket draws from James Harden's career: that consistency over a decades-long horizon matters more than peak achievement, and that showing up — every game, every day — is itself the win. The principle decouples worth from trophies.

## Origin

Sriket started watching Houston Rockets basketball in 2012, the year Harden was traded to Houston. The traits that imprinted on him weren't the highlight reels — they were the durability. Harden played as many games as anyone, dropped 30+ nightly, did it for nearly two decades, and still does it.

A YouTube video on Harden's work ethic is the artifact Sriket points to as the inspiration: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMBQJkwpkqo>.

## The principle as Sriket reads it

> He works for 20 years straight. He might never win a chip but he showed up for me every day. He is one of the most winningest players of all time. He was able to provide for his mom.

Three claims compressed into a few lines:

1. **Showing up is the work.** Not flashes of brilliance. The act of being present and producing, night after night, season after season.
2. **A title is not the metric.** Harden may never win a championship. By the chip-only metric, his career is incomplete. By the show-up metric, his career is a masterclass.
3. **Providing for your family is the real outcome.** Harden was able to take care of his mother. That is what the work was for. Provision converts effort into love.

## How it connects to the rest of Sriket's stack

The Harden principle stacks naturally on top of [[honor-the-sacrifice]]: his mother sacrificed everything, the response is to show up every day for decades and convert that work into provision and dignity for her. It connects to [[software-momentum-based]] as a tactical corollary — showing up requires consecutive, protected work blocks, not heroic sprints. It also reframes the Houston connection from [[childhood-nomadic]] (born in Houston) into something Sriket actively roots for: the Rockets are not just a team but the carrier of a principle.

## What the principle prescribes

- Don't optimize for the trophy that may never come.
- Optimize for the streak.
- Measure yourself by the games you didn't miss, not the games you won.
- Make sure the work, eventually, becomes provision for someone you love.
