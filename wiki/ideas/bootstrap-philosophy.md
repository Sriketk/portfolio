Sriket's stance on funding his startup work. The claim: raising money is the default-bad option, and the case for raising has to overcome a strong presumption in favor of bootstrapping.

## What he means

"You probably shouldn't raise money if you don't need to."

The stance is twofold:
- **Philosophical**: he wants to build a real business organically, not one optimized around the next round.
- **Defensive**: he kept hearing horror stories from tech Twitter about founders who took money they didn't need and lost control of their direction, their timelines, or their companies.

## Origin

The belief formed during [[queens-era]] as he consumed more and more tech Twitter. Over time he grew tired of online culture glorifying fundraising over building actual products. The bootstrap stance is his explicit reaction to that pattern.

## How it shows up in practice

Sriket bootstrapped [[cobalt]] through [[solo-builder-era]] and into [[queens-era]]. The stance shaped what he was willing to spend on, how fast he was willing to grow, and what kinds of opportunities he chased — none of which depended on external capital.

## Open question

The shift signaled by [[great-things-not-done-alone]] — Sriket looking to join a team — may or may not be compatible with the bootstrap stance. Joining a funded team is a different fact pattern than raising your own round. The wiki does not yet resolve whether the bootstrap stance survives the team shift or is recontextualized as "I won't raise alone."
