A rejection of the default cultural script that a person should have one occupation and one hobby. Sriket's claim: life is wide enough and short enough that pursuing many things — equally seriously — is the correct response, not a failure of focus.

## The default he's rejecting

The accustomed shape: one job, one hobby, everything else is filler. Sriket sees this as a needless contraction. There are too many interesting things in the world to accept that ceiling.

## What he wants to pursue

In his own framing, all on the same tier:

- Build software (the current main thread)
- Get better at basketball
- Become a better cook
- Learn new languages
- Get better at calisthenics
- Read

The list is not a hierarchy. The operative word is "equally." Each of these is approached as a real thing to get better at, not as a side amusement to whatever the "real" pursuit is.

## Why the principle is non-trivial

The standard objection: you can't be great at many things, so spreading attention across all of them produces mediocrity everywhere. Sriket's implicit counter is that "greatness" as a single-domain summit is the wrong success function. The right one is: a life that contains many serious practices.

This compounds with [[show-up-every-day]] — showing up consistently across many domains, year over year, produces a different kind of capability than peak performance in one. Basketball was already a load-bearing part of [[maryland-college]] and the eighth-grade fitness turn in [[charlotte-era]] — Sriket has a track record of taking second-tier pursuits seriously.

## What the principle prescribes

- Don't apologize for spreading attention.
- Treat hobbies as real practices, not as breaks from the real work.
- Measure progress in each domain on its own terms.
- Reject "you should focus on one thing" as the default advice; require evidence that focus is the right move in a given moment.
